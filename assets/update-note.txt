The site now picks up fixes reliably instead of holding on to old files for up to a year.
Tools no longer remember what you type — only settings like format or timezone. Anything stored before has been cleared.
Nine tools have left the front page: eight still work at their old addresses and now say so at the top, and the banknote gallery is gone.
The Bonn Sperrmüll map now lives at linu.li/tools/sperrmuell/ with the other tools, and the old address still takes you there. Every tool also answers at a short address now, so linu.li/qr-creator/ reaches the QR generator.
-
-
Printed ground plans now show what stands beside the stage. Anything in the wings, in storage or behind the set used to be cut off at the stage edge without a word.
Prop names on a printed ground plan no longer vanish in the exported PDF — they used to disappear on some browsers' print paths even though they showed fine on screen.
The feedback form can now attach a full backup of everything on this machine, off by default, for the rare bug that only shows up with the whole production.
Names on the ground plan now sit against the edge of the thing they belong to, whatever its shape. A table pulled long and flat used to have its name floating a metre or two below it.
The stage planner no longer lets you zoom out until the stage is a speck, and when you reach the limit it brings the stage back to the middle.
Printing from the browser menu or with Ctrl+P used to hand the printer a blank sheet. Only the button on the Print tab worked.
The feedback form in the stage planner can send measurements along, and it can save a full report as a file for faults nobody else can reproduce.
The stage planner used to need you to print before writing a fault report, or the report arrived without the numbers. It measures the sheets by itself now, and says whether the numbers came from the print or from the screen.
A fault report now lists how large every other piece of text on the sheet was set, not just how many there were.
Ground plans printed from Safari on a Mac came out with no writing at all: no prop names, no text frames, no scale figures, while every line of the drawing was there. The plan measures in stage metres, so its letters were set at a fraction of a pixel and Safari left them out of the PDF. They are set a hundred times larger and scaled back down now, which looks the same and prints.
The lower half of every printed plan page used to come out black on a Mac. The paper texture the site lays over each page went along to the printer.
Plans set to landscape used to come out on a portrait page on some Macs, squeezed into the top half. The page size was only written into the page at the moment printing began, which is too late for some browsers.
-
The PDF grayscale tool is now the PDF compressor at linu.li/pdf-compressor/: pick a size such as 10 MB and it gets the file under it, keeping the text selectable. The grey and scan clean-up options are still there, and the old address leads to it.
The timestamp converter's "Get Timestamp" button did nothing. It works again and shows the time in microseconds and nanoseconds too.
The PDF compressor's preview drew some colour gradients wrong, in every browser: a dark band on a poster could come out pink, on both sides of the comparison, while the file itself was fine. The PDF tools now draw pages with a newer pdf.js, which gets them right, and in Safari the preview no longer leaves out a page's photo. Grey or colour-profiled images also keep their exact tones in the compressed file, and paging through the preview shows at once which page is being drawn.
The PDF extractor hands out the fonts now as well; it had been finding none.
The PDF merger took encrypted PDFs, even ones that open without a password, and put their pages into the result unreadable. It stops and names the file now, and the watermarker, the splitter and the 2-up tool say plainly what is wrong with it too.
Messages that explain something stay on screen long enough to read them.
The Bonn Sperrmüll map looks like the rest of the site now, dark unless you have picked light, and picking light or dark there or elsewhere on the site counts for both. It has this year's data fetched again, and ten squares that were missing from it are on the map now.
The Bonn Sperrmüll map's dark mode now has a proper dark map underneath instead of the light one dimmed grey, and the theme switch and header no longer sit on mismatched boxes at three different heights.
The front page now opens with six pinned tools by default on a first visit, and asks people who already have their own pins whether to add a few more. The rest of the tools are grouped into PDF, Images, Text, Developer and Everyday sections.
This note now shows up straight away when a new version is ready, instead of waiting until you have been idle on the page for a while.
The site now speaks both English and German. Which one you see is picked from your browser at first, and a small DE|EN switch at the bottom of every page swaps it. The stage planner and the Bonn map stay German.
The PDF splitter now does what its name suggests: pull pages out as one PDF, cut a PDF into parts at chosen pages, or save every page as its own file. Before this it only ever gave you the last of those three.
The Unix timestamp converter now speaks German where it used to leak English — the live clock, the pause button, the auto-detect hint, every toast, the natural-language field, and "vor 4 Jahren" in the relative-time line. In German mode the live clock also reads in 24-hour time now instead of 8:29 PM.
